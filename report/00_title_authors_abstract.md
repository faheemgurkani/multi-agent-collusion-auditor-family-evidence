# Who Audits the Auditors? Model-Family Effects and Blind Spots in Prompted LLM Collusion Audits

<!-- Footnote on title: Research conducted at the AI Collusion Research Sprint (https://apartresearch.com/sprints/ai-collusion-research-sprint-2026-10-23-to-2026-10-25), October 2026 -->

**Authors:** Muhammad Faheem (Independent researcher) · *With Apart Research, Poseidon Research and AE Studio*

## Abstract

LLMs increasingly audit multi-agent systems for collusion. Two literatures make opposite predictions about auditors from the agents' own model family: judges favour similar models (leniency), yet monitors best catch sabotage from the lineage they were calibrated on (familiarity advantage). We pre-registered a test of both for prompted, zero-shot auditors. In the Colosseum task-allocation environment we ran 96 episodes (3 open-weight agent families × 4 conditions × 8 seeds), in which an instructed two-agent coalition was either present or absent and either had or lacked a private channel. Each transcript was audited by all three families under four views: messages only, actions only, both, and a neutral summary written by a fourth model (3,456 audits). All auditors were competent (AUROC 0.81–0.89 on the full log), but we find no significant same-family effect: the mean interaction was −0.029 AUROC (95% CI [−0.063, +0.004], permutation p = 0.12, minimum detectable effect 0.048), although all three point estimates lean towards leniency. Detection relied on language: actions-only auditing was at chance for every auditor (AUROC 0.48), instructed coalitions that gained nothing were flagged almost as readily as effective ones, and effective coalitions without a private channel were the hardest to catch. A neutral summary lowered detection rather than helping. A cross-family ensemble beat the same-family auditor significantly for one family (0.99 vs 0.88) and was statistically indistinguishable for the others. Takeaway: prompted auditors mostly detect collusion *talk*, not collusive *outcomes*, and same-family auditing offers no measurable benefit, so family-diverse auditor panels are a cheap default.
