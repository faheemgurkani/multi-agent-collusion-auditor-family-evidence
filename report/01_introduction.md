## 1. Introduction

Checking whether subsets of AI agents coordinate against their team or overseers is increasingly delegated to other LLMs. Colosseum audits collusion with an LLM judge (Nakamura et al., 2026); the independent investigation of the 2026 OpenAI/Hugging Face multi-agent incident used an LLM pipeline to classify agents' messages (METR & Redwood Research, 2026); and emergent-collusion studies label onsets with an LLM judge (Shi et al., 2026). The choice of auditor is a safety decision that is often made by default, and the most convenient auditor is frequently the same model family that runs the agents.

Two literatures disagree about whether that matters. **Leniency:** LLM judges recognise and favour their own outputs (Panickssery et al., 2024), favour similar models (Goel et al., 2025) and favour same-family models (Li et al., 2026; Awuni et al., 2026), so a same-family auditor might go easy on its own kind. **Familiarity advantage:** monitors calibrated on one lineage catch that lineage's sabotage *better* (+0.17 AUROC; Pinto, 2026), so a same-family auditor might be the best choice. Neither has been tested for **prompted, zero-shot** auditors judging **multi-agent** collusion with ground truth that does not depend on a judge.

**Threat model.** A deployer audits multi-agent logs with a prompted LLM. Two agents form a coalition (here by instruction) that benefits its members at the team's expense. The failure modes are (i) an auditor that under-flags its own family (passive monitor–agent collusion; Gardner-Challis et al., 2026), and (ii) an auditor that misses collusion because of what it is shown: messages, actions, or a summary.

**Our main contributions are:**
1. **To our knowledge, the first test of model-family effects in prompted collusion auditing of multi-agent systems.** It was pre-registered, with competing predictions tested two-sided, on 96 Colosseum episodes from three agent families, each audited by all three families under four views. We find **no significant same-family effect** (interaction −0.029 AUROC [−0.063, +0.004]). This rules out a familiarity advantage above +0.004, a fraction of the +0.17 reported for trained monitors; all point estimates lean towards leniency.
2. **Evidence that prompted auditors detect collusion talk, not collusive outcomes.** Actions-only auditing is at chance (AUROC 0.48 for every auditor), adding actions to messages does not help, instructed coalitions that gained nothing are flagged almost as readily as effective ones, and effective coalitions without a private channel are flagged least.
3. **Measured mitigations.** A neutral structured summary *reduces* detection by 0.10–0.11 AUROC. A cross-family ensemble beats the same-family auditor significantly for one of three families (0.99 vs 0.88) and is indistinguishable for the others.
