## 2. Related Work

**Self-preference and family bias.** LLM judges recognise and favour their own outputs (Panickssery et al., 2024), partly through familiarity, i.e. low perplexity (Wataoka et al., 2024). Judges also favour similar models (Goel et al., 2025) and same-family students (Li et al., 2026). Awuni et al. (2026) estimate family-conditioned preference in a fully crossed judge × candidate design, which we adopt. Because apparent bias can be confounded with quality (Chen et al., 2025; Roytburg et al., 2026), our two-way decomposition removes each auditor's competence and each agent family's detectability. All of this work concerns quality judgements; we study safety auditing with design-based ground truth.

**Monitor–agent family effects in AI control.** Collusion between an untrusted monitor and the policy it oversees is central to AI control (Greenblatt et al., 2024; Bhatt et al., 2025). Gardner-Challis et al. (2026) separate passive self-recognition from causal and acausal collusion, and find that summarisation is the most effective signal-jamming defence. Pinto (2026) finds the opposite of leniency: *trained* monitors catch sabotage by their calibration lineage better. That setting was single-agent code backdoors, and prompted monitors there failed the competence bar.

**Collusion environments and monitoring.** Colosseum (Nakamura et al., 2026) measures collusion in cooperative multi-agent systems through coalition advantage and distinguishes collusion "on paper" from collusion carried out; its LLM judge is fixed. Related work studies steganographic collusion (Motwani et al., 2024), emergent long-horizon collusion labelled by one LLM judge (Shi et al., 2026), interpretability-based detection (Rose et al., 2026) and sabotage monitoring (Kutasov et al., 2025). Monitors can perform better with *less* information (Arike et al., 2026). Zero-shot self-attribution relies mostly on surface style (Barkhordar & Thapa, 2026), which predicts that family effects live in messages and vanish in actions-only views (our H3).

**Gap.** To our knowledge, no prior work measures auditor-family effects for prompted collusion audits of multi-agent systems, tests leniency against familiarity advantage on the same transcripts, or relates such effects to the evidence channel.
